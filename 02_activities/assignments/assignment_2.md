     Bad Visualization: Flow Diagram

     Aesthetic Qualities - The colour palette is simple and not overwhelming, however the visualization does not include any labels, legends, or information regarding what the colours mean or what the visualization is actually trying to represent or communicate. 

     Substantive Qualities - There is no source and no labels, making it impossible to tell what is being represented. Furthermore, the flows are landing on random places on the globe without labels of direction or their origin/destination, yet other cities, unrelated to the flows, are labelled. There is no way to discern if the visual is substantive or accurate because so much information and context is missing.

     Perceptual Qualities - The lack of information (labels, title, legend, source, context, etc.) makes it impossible to discern what is being communicated. 


     Good (Better) Visualization: Choropleth Map

     Aesthetic Qualities - The map has a distinct colour palette and good use of white space and graphic design principles to organize the information (e.g., title and text hierarchy). The use of the figure-ground style of representation also makes it clear that the info is related to the built form in New York City. 

     Substantive Qualities - The map includes a legend, units, and a disclaimer based on same of the missing data, although the source is not directly included on the visualization. 

     Perceptual Qualities - It is clear that the goal of the visualization is to show the distribution of land price across Manhattan, and where the most expensive land is located (i.e., in Lower Manhattan and around Broadway). This would be a good map to overlay with other information, such as average income, to investigate the relationships between land price and other variables.
